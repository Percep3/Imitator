\title{Restoring Temporal Order in Autoregressive Video-to-Token Sign Transcription}
\author{Anonymous authors}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
We study direct transcription of sign-keypoint streams into ordered language-model
token IDs, without an intermediate gloss classifier at inference time.  An initial
autoregressive system identified much of the sign content but behaved as a bag of
signs: its visual Transformer had no global positional signal, and segment
permutation left 86.3\% of predictions unchanged.  We introduce \system, a compact
autoregressive video-to-token model that adds sinusoidal temporal position to the
visual memory, restricts the output softmax to the 121 \Gemma{} IDs present in the
task, removes label smoothing, and selects checkpoints using inner-validation edit
similarity.  On three development folds, these changes raise strict sequence
accuracy from roughly 2\% to 16.9--19.8\% and pairwise gloss-order accuracy from
chance to approximately 0.99.  We prospectively evaluate the frozen configuration
on two signer-held-out folds.  Confirmatory strict accuracy is
\FoldSevenExact\% and \FoldEightExact\%, with paired permutation drops of
\FoldSevenPermutationDrop{} and \FoldEightPermutationDrop{} in token edit
similarity.  The claims are restricted to synthetic continuous streams formed by
randomly concatenating isolated signs; real continuous signing remains out of
scope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Continuous sign transcription requires both identifying signed content and preserving
its temporal order.  This distinction is easy to obscure when evaluation emphasizes
token-level averages: a model may recover many correct units while producing a globally
misordered sequence.  The issue is especially consequential for an autoregressive
decoder, where an early ordering error changes the prefix conditioning every later
prediction.

We consider a deliberately constrained task: 2D keypoint sequences are assembled by
concatenating isolated signs, and the model emits the corresponding sequence of
subword IDs from the \Gemma{} tokenizer.  The target order is randomized by the data
generator.  This removes a linguistic-order shortcut and makes successful ordering
direct evidence that the model uses temporal video information, while also limiting
the ecological scope of the experiment.

Our initial decoder was nearly invariant to segment order.  A code and intervention
audit traced the failure to a missing positional encoding in the visual Transformer.
The local temporal convolution encoded short-range motion, but the memory exposed to
cross-attention had no representation of global frame position.  Adding position,
together with a task-effective output vocabulary and a corrected optimization regime,
produced a large development-set improvement without changing the token-level product
interface.

This paper makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We isolate a bag-of-signs failure through a segment-permutation intervention
          and connect it to the permutation equivariance of a Transformer encoder
          without positional information.
    \item We present \system, a direct autoregressive video-to-\Gemma-token model whose
          visual memory preserves global temporal order.
    \item We evaluate a configuration frozen on development folds under a hash-verified,
          prospectively registered signer-held-out protocol, including paired baseline
          tests and a causal order intervention.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Skeleton-based sign recognition.}
Spatial--temporal graph networks provide a compact way to model joints and motion
\citep{yan2018stgcn}.  Recent skeleton CSLR systems study input decomposition,
multi-stream static/motion features, temporal downsampling, and signer generalization
\citep{min2025skeleton,nam2026liftsign}.  Multi-scale spatial--temporal enhancement
and explicit frame-level objectives likewise emphasize that preserving fine temporal
information is central to continuous recognition \citep{wang2025stnet}.  Other systems
combine body, handshape, velocity, or non-manual streams
\citep{zhao2026multimodal,harrouch2026manual,yenisari2025multifeature}, or connect an
ST-GCN recognizer to a language-generation stage \citep{pai2026tsl}.  This literature
also identifies recurring threats relevant here: pose-estimation error, scarce
signer-diverse data, coarticulation, and overfitting in small Transformer datasets
\citep{harrouch2026manual,min2025skeleton,nam2026liftsign,huamaniLessIsMore}.

\paragraph{Connecting visual or acoustic encoders to language models.}
Gloss-free sign translation increasingly connects visual representations to pretrained
language models.  Sign2GPT uses pseudo-gloss pretraining and LLM-based generation
\citep{wong2024sign2gpt}; SignLLM constructs discrete, hierarchical sign tokens for a
frozen LLM \citep{gong2024signllm}; and FLa-LLM separates visual initialization from
LLM fine-tuning to prevent the language model from dominating visual learning
\citep{chen2024flallm}.  Related speech work maps encoder information into an LLM token
space through continuous prompts or CTC posterior-weighted token embeddings
\citep{deng2025wav2prompt,ma2025legoslm}.  These works motivate a token-level interface,
but our controlled recognition task does not perform natural-language translation or
invoke an LLM at inference time.  Moreover, contextual embedding spaces can be strongly
anisotropic \citep{ethayarajh2019contextual}; consequently, we do not interpret embedding
cosine geometry alone as evidence of visual--linguistic alignment.

\paragraph{Alignment objectives.}
Connectionist temporal classification (CTC) \citep{graves2006ctc} remains a standard
choice for monotonic weak alignment in continuous recognition
\citep{min2025skeleton,nam2026liftsign}.  Joint CTC/attention has also been adapted to
text-level sign translation where the output order need not be monotonic with video
\citep{tan2025textctc}; an earlier end-to-end Sign Language Transformer coupled CSLR and
translation through CTC supervision \citep{camgoz2020signtransformers}.  Boundary-aware
systems instead use explicit handshape and motion
cues to segment signs \citep{zhao2026multimodal}.  In our development study, a token-level
CTC auxiliary head was evaluated but not retained: it did not improve the autoregressive
model, and its standalone decoding collapsed to blank.  The central gain instead came
from restoring global temporal position to the visual memory.

\section{Task and Data}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Inputs.}
Each example is a sequence $X\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times111\times2}$ of normalized 2D
keypoints.  The synthetic generator samples between two and eight isolated clips and
concatenates them, inserting zero-valued neutral gaps of zero to eight frames.  Clip
order is sampled independently for every synthetic example.

\paragraph{Targets.}
The 64 gloss labels are tokenized with the fixed \Gemma{} tokenizer.  A target is the
concatenation of the tokenizations of its sampled glosses.  Across this task there are
118 content-token IDs; adding BOS, EOS, and PAD yields an effective vocabulary of 121
real \Gemma{} IDs.  The model therefore remains a token-ID transcriber rather than a
64-way gloss classifier.

\paragraph{Signer-held-out protocol.}
The dataset contains ten signers.  Each LOSO fold excludes one outer-test signer and
uses a second signer for inner validation; all remaining signers form the training set.
Folds 1--6 are development folds.  Folds 7 and 8 are the prospectively registered
confirmation folds; folds 9 and 10 remain reserved.  Each outer evaluation contains
896 deterministic synthetic sequences generated with seed 200023.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Visual encoder}

The frame encoder applies three ST-GCN blocks to the keypoint graph, projects to 128
channels, averages over graph nodes, and applies two temporal convolution blocks.  A
one-layer, four-head Transformer encoder then produces visual memory
$H\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times128}$.  The ST-GCN projection is initialized from a
signer-clean CIF checkpoint whose lineage is verified by SHA-256 and signer lists.

The original encoder passed temporal-convolution features directly to the Transformer.
Because self-attention without position is permutation equivariant, its global memory
could not distinguish segment order beyond the local convolutional receptive field.
Following the explicit positional construction of the original Transformer
\citep{vaswani2017attention}, \system{} adds a fixed sinusoidal vector $p_t$ before
self-attention,
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{h}_t = h_t + p_t,
\end{equation}
thereby making global frame position available to the encoder and downstream
cross-attention.

\subsection{Autoregressive token decoder}

The causal decoder has two Transformer layers, four attention heads, hidden size 128,
and feed-forward size 512.  At step $i$, it predicts a \Gemma{} token conditioned on the
visual memory and the gold prefix during training,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{AR}}
      = -\sum_i \log p_\theta(y_i\mid y_{<i}, X).
\end{equation}
The input embedding and output projection are tied.  Greedy inference begins with BOS,
stops on EOS, forbids EOS at the first step, and emits at most 32 content tokens.

Rather than allocating the full 262,400-row \Gemma{} softmax, we use a bijective dense
reparameterization over the 121 IDs effective in this task.  Every output row maps to a
specific \Gemma{} ID, and decoding maps it back before evaluation.  This reduces unused
parameters without changing the external target space.  The tied token embedding and
output rows are initialized from the corresponding classifier rows of the same
signer-clean CIF checkpoint; all lineage and shape checks occur before training.

\subsection{Training configuration}

The frozen E1 configuration uses seed 23, 2,048 synthetic training examples per epoch,
and 512 deterministic inner-validation examples.  The decoder is optimized for 30
epochs at learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$.  The visual TCN and Transformer remain frozen
for five epochs and then train at $3\times10^{-5}$; the ST-GCN and spatial projection
remain frozen throughout.  Label smoothing and auxiliary CTC loss are zero.  The closed
checkpoint is selected lexicographically by inner-validation token edit similarity and
then strict accuracy.  No outer-test result participates in model or epoch selection.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\paragraph{Primary metrics.}
Strict accuracy is one only when the complete predicted ID sequence equals the target
and EOS is emitted.  Token edit similarity is
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{EditSim}(\hat{y},y)
      = \max\left(0,1-\frac{d_{\mathrm{Lev}}(\hat{y},y)}{|y|}\right).
\end{equation}
We report sample-level nonparametric percentile bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals
\citep{efron1979bootstrap} with 10,000 fixed-seed replicates.

\paragraph{CIF comparator.}
CIF is not a candidate final architecture; it is the historical monotonic baseline and
the source of signer-clean encoder initialization.  Its affine count calibration is fit
only on inner validation and then frozen.  E1 and CIF are evaluated on identical outer
samples.  For strict accuracy, we count E1-only successes as wins and CIF-only successes
as losses, omit ties, and apply an exact two-sided sign test.

\paragraph{Order metric.}
Predicted token sequences are greedily parsed into gloss units by longest token match.
A gloss is eligible when it occurs exactly once in both prediction and target.  For
every pair of eligible glosses, we test whether their relative order agrees and
micro-average concordant pairs.  Samples with fewer than two eligible glosses contribute
no order pairs.  The implementation is versioned and reproduces the original audit
metric exactly.

\paragraph{Causal permutation test.}
Using gold synthetic boundaries, we permute complete video segments while preserving
all frames, sequence length, and the original target.  A non-identity permutation is
forced for every sample.  We report the paired mean decrease in edit similarity between
clean and permuted inputs.  This is an intervention on visual order, not a data
augmentation used during training.

\subsection{Prospectively registered hypotheses}

All criteria are conjunctive across folds 7 and 8:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{H1:} E1 has more strict-accuracy wins than losses against affine CIF,
          with two-sided sign-test $p<0.01$, in each fold.
    \item \textbf{H2:} clean strict accuracy is at least 11.9\% in each fold.  The bound
          is unilateral; performance above the development range is a success.
    \item \textbf{H3:} pairwise gloss-order accuracy is at least 0.90 and the absolute
          paired permutation drop in edit similarity is at least 0.15 in each fold.
\end{itemize}
The complete confirmation succeeds only if all three hypotheses pass in both folds.

\section{Discussion}

The development intervention supports a specific architectural explanation rather than
a generic capacity claim.  The autoregressive decoder could already identify a useful
subset of signed content, but without global temporal position its cross-attention read
an effectively unordered memory.  Position restores the information required to bind
content to sequence order.  Restricting the softmax and correcting checkpoint selection
improve optimization efficiency, but neither alone explains sensitivity to segment
permutation.

Random target order is both a strength and a limitation.  It prevents a language model
from guessing the next sign and makes the permutation test unusually clean.  Conversely,
it removes the linguistic structure and coarticulation present in natural continuous
signing.  The present evidence should therefore be interpreted as controlled visual
sequence transcription, not end-to-end natural sign-language understanding.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

Only one training seed is used, so between-seed variance is not estimated.  The
confirmatory sample contains two outer signers; sample-level confidence intervals do not
substitute for broader signer-level replication.  All sequences are synthetic
concatenations of isolated clips, neutral gaps are artificial, and gold boundaries are
available only to the diagnostic permutation procedure.  Folds 4--6 influenced the E1
configuration and H2 threshold and are labeled development throughout.  Signers can
serve as inner validation in one rotation and outer test in another, although every
model is trained independently and excludes its own outer signer.  Finally, the
effective vocabulary covers this 64-gloss task and does not establish scalability to
open-vocabulary transcription.  These restrictions are material: isolated-sign systems
do not capture continuous coarticulation \citep{harrouch2026manual}, skeleton CSLR can
generalize poorly when training data are scarce \citep{min2025skeleton}, and keypoint
pipelines remain dependent on pose-estimation quality even when they reduce RGB nuisance
variation \citep{harrouch2026manual,nam2026liftsign}.

\section{Reproducibility and Audit Trail}

The pre-registration is committed before E1 outer evaluation on folds 7--8.  Data splits,
source-checkpoint lineage, source and closed-checkpoint SHA-256 hashes, per-sample
predictions, paired comparisons, bootstrap seeds, and hypothesis decisions are stored in
machine-readable JSON.  The time-budget amendment postpones secondary perturbations and
exploratory probes but does not alter E1, H1--H3, or their decision rules.  Exact commands
and hashes are included in the accompanying repository.
