\section{Limitations and Scope}

The study uses synthetic concatenations of isolated LSA64 clips, not naturally
continuous signing.  It contains no coarticulation, natural sentence order, or
open-vocabulary text; the 121-row output layer covers only IDs observed in this
task.  Labels are Spanish lexical annotations of LSA signs, so direct token
prediction is not equivalent to modeling LSA grammar.  Skeleton inputs omit visual
appearance and remain dependent on pose-estimation quality; isolated-sign findings
cannot simply be transferred to continuous signing
\citep{harrouch2026manual,nam2026liftsign}.

Only seed 23 is used, between-seed variance is unknown, and only two outer signers
are prospectively confirmatory.  Sample-level bootstrap intervals do not replace
signer-level replication.  E1 and the H2 threshold were developed on folds 4--6.
The tokenizer restriction is efficient for a closed label set but must be expanded
and revalidated for sentence data.  Finally, the correction audit tests one model
and a fixed prompt; its defensible conclusion is limited to the tested setup and
rates, although the absence of contextual information is a structural limitation
of the present data.
