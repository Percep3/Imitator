\system{} connects a lightweight visual model to a pretrained language model
by predicting explicit \Gemma{} token IDs in place of the original system's
continuous embeddings.  We first ask whether this interface can recover the
exact tokens of one isolated LSA64 sign from an unseen signer.  Strict accuracy
for the replicas is 13.75\% and 10.94\% on folds 7 and 8, respectively.  Toward
the original goal of sentence translation, we then test how well the interface
transcribes synthetic streams of two to eight signs in order.  On three
development signers, strict accuracy improves from 1.8--2.3\% for the initial
autoregressive decoder to 16.9--19.8\%.  The original checkpoints achieve
\FoldSevenExact\% and \FoldEightExact\% on the two prospectively held-out
signers.  In a separate exploratory audit without sentence context, \Gemma{} 3n
preserves 95\% of clean isolated labels, recovers no exact sequence at the
tested nonzero substitution rates, and slightly decreases chrF.  The experiments
address controlled sign transcription. Natural sentence translation remains
untested.
